# Starling — submission

**One sentence.** Starling is a native iOS news reader that generates the words and the page design of every story at runtime around the person reading it (their attention, pulse, movement, time of day and chosen sources), proposes each adaptation for the reader to accept, correct or regenerate, and does it all inside one fixed design genome.

**One paragraph.** Every interface we ship is a guess frozen at release; Starling unfreezes one. When you open a story the phone already knows whether you are looking, walking, tense or settled (ARKit gaze, Apple Watch or fingertip pulse, CoreMotion, the clock), and a schema-constrained model returns an *Edition*: the rewritten text and a page design chosen from a closed vocabulary of palettes, typefaces, scales, blocks and five layout archetypes, with a one-sentence justification you can read. Cards lead with generated posters that carry the headline and the story's key number as typography in the image. Nothing changes without you: the page never swaps on its own, and Keep, Regenerate, "Not how I feel" and the per-state layout rules you edit in Settings are fed into every later generation, so the interface evolves per reader. Two generations of the same story are one tap apart (Calm / Focused, Regenerate, Compare). You can also call the story: an ElevenLabs newsreader greets you by name, tells it in two sentences, hands over, and asks before changing what is on screen. Twenty sample stories ship pre-generated so the demo is instant and offline; everything else generates live. Structure, content and design exist only because they were generated for this person, in this moment — the interface is never finished.
